Privacy Policy

Sin Bin: Rugby Rules Quiz

Last updated 18 November 2026

Who we are

Sin Bin: Rugby Rules Quiz ("Sin Bin", "the app") is a quiz app that helps rugby union referees learn the Laws of the Game. It is made by Adam Malik, an individual based in the United Kingdom. In this policy, "we", "us" and "our" mean Adam Malik.

We are the data controller for the personal information described in this policy. If you have any question about it, or want to use any of your rights, email us at sportlawapps@gmail.com.

The short version

  • There are no accounts. We never ask for your name, and we do not know who you are.
  • Your quiz answers, progress and settings stay on your phone. We cannot see them.
  • There are no analytics or tracking tools of our own in the app.
  • The free version shows ads from Google AdMob. Ads are only personalised for adults who agree to it. Under-18s never get personalised ads.
  • If you report a question or send us a message, we receive what you write so we can act on it.
  • We do not sell your personal information.

Information that stays on your phone

The app keeps the following on your phone, in its own private storage:

  • Your quiz history: which questions you have seen and answered, whether you got them right, and when.
  • Your progress figures and any progress you have reset.
  • Your settings: the age band you chose, light or dark mode, and whether you have bought No Ads.
  • Reports and messages waiting to be sent when you have no signal.
  • The ad consent choices you made (stored by Google's consent tool, described below).

None of this is sent to us. It stays until you reset your progress, clear the app's data or delete the app. Your phone may include it in its own backup (Android backup to your Google account, or an iCloud or computer backup on iPhone), which you control in your phone's settings. We cannot see those backups.

The age band question

When you first open the app, it asks whether you are under 13, 13 to 17, or 18 or over. You do not have to answer; if you skip it, the app treats you as under 18. The answer is stored only on your phone and you can change it in Settings.

It is used for one thing: deciding how ads are shown. The app tells Google's ad service to treat requests from under-18s as coming from a child or teenager, which turns off personalised ads. Your actual age band is never sent anywhere.

Information sent when you use the app

Question updates. The app downloads new and corrected questions from a website we host on GitHub Pages, a service run by GitHub, Inc. in the United States. As with any website, GitHub receives your phone's IP address and basic technical details (such as the type of device) when the app downloads a file. We do not receive these, and GitHub uses them to run and protect its service.

GitHub General Privacy Statement

Reporting an incorrect question. If you report a question, we receive: the reason you chose, the comment you wrote, which question it was, the version of the questions and of the app you were using, and the time. Please do not put personal details in your comment. Reports are sent to a small program of ours (a Google Apps Script) that saves them in a private Google Sheet and emails them to our inbox (Gmail). All three are run by Google, acting on our behalf. Google receives your phone's IP address when a report is sent, as part of running the service; we do not.

Contacting us. If you send us a message from the app, we receive your message and, only if you choose to give it, an email address to reply to. It is delivered in the same way as reports. We use it only to answer you.

If you email us directly, we receive your email address and whatever you write, and use it only to reply.

Advertising

The free version of Sin Bin shows a full-screen ad after some completed quizzes, never in the middle of one. Ads are provided by Google AdMob, run by Google LLC and Google Ireland Limited. Google decides which ad to show, and for that it collects information from your phone. According to Google, this can include:

  • your device's advertising identifier (on Android, the advertising ID; on iPhone, the identifier for advertisers, only if you allow tracking);
  • your IP address, and your approximate location worked out from it (never your precise location);
  • details of your device and the app, such as the model, operating system and language;
  • how you interact with ads, and diagnostic information such as crash and performance data.

Google uses this to show ads, measure them, prevent fraud and improve its services. Google acts as a separate data controller for this; its use is covered by Google's own privacy policy.

How Google uses information from apps that use its services

Google Privacy Policy

Personalised and non-personalised ads:

  • Under 13 (or if you have not given an age band and the app treats you as under 18): ads are never personalised, Google is told to treat the request as child-directed, and the app never asks to track you.
  • 13 to 17: ads are never personalised, and the app never asks to track you.
  • 18 or over: after your first completed quiz, the app explains why it is asking, then shows Google's consent form (and on iPhone, Apple's tracking request). Ads are personalised only if you agree. If you say no, you still see ads, but they are not based on your interests.

You can change your ad choices at any time in Settings, under Privacy options. On your phone you can also reset or delete your advertising ID (on Android, in your phone's privacy settings under Ads) or turn off tracking for the app (on iPhone, in Settings, Privacy & Security, Tracking).

If you buy No Ads, the app stops requesting ads altogether, and the ad consent form and tracking request are no longer shown.

Buying No Ads

The No Ads purchase is handled entirely by the Apple App Store or Google Play. We never receive your name, email address or payment details. The store only tells the app that the purchase has been made, so it can switch ads off and restore your purchase on a new phone.

Apple Privacy Policy

If you bought No Ads on one platform and ask us to move it to the other, you send us your store order confirmation by email. We use it only to check the purchase and send you a code, and then delete it.

What the app does not do

  • It has no accounts, sign-in, profiles or messaging.
  • It does not ask for access to your camera, microphone, photos, contacts or location.
  • It contains no analytics, crash reporting or tracking tools of our own.
  • It does not sell your personal information, or share it except as described in this policy.

Apple and Google may give us summary statistics about downloads, use and crashes, but only if you have allowed your phone to share them. These figures are combined and do not identify you.

Our legal reasons for using your information

Under United Kingdom data protection law, we rely on:

  • Consent, for personalised ads and for storing or reading advertising information on your phone. You can withdraw it at any time in Settings, under Privacy options.
  • Our legitimate interests, for keeping the questions up to date, fixing questions you report, answering your messages, and showing non-personalised ads that keep the app free. We have weighed these against your interests, and kept the information involved to a minimum.
  • Performing a contract with you, for checking a purchase you ask us to move between platforms.

How long we keep information

  • Information on your phone: until you reset your progress, clear the app's data or delete the app.
  • Reports and messages you send us: in our private Google Sheet and our inbox, until we have dealt with them and no longer than 12 months after that.
  • Store order confirmations sent for a platform transfer: deleted once the transfer is done, and within 3 months at the latest.
  • Information collected by Google, GitHub, Apple or Google Play: as set out in their own privacy policies.

Where information is processed

Some of the services named in this policy are based in, or process information in, countries outside the United Kingdom: Google and GitHub in the United States. Where that happens, the transfer is protected as UK law requires, for example by the UK–US data bridge or by contract terms approved for the purpose.

Keeping information safe

Everything the app sends or receives travels over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection. Information on your phone is kept in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read. No system is perfectly secure, but we keep what we collect to a minimum so there is very little to protect.

Children and young referees

Sin Bin is a learning tool for rugby referees, and in England referees can qualify from the age of 14, so we expect some users to be under 18. The app is not designed for children under 13.

For anyone who says they are under 18, or who does not say, the app never shows personalised ads and never asks to track them. Their age band stays on their phone. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13; if you believe a child under 13 has sent us personal information, such as an email address in a message, email us and we will delete it.

Your rights

Under United Kingdom data protection law you have the right to ask for a copy of the personal information we hold about you, and to ask us to correct it, delete it, limit how we use it, or stop using it. Where we rely on your consent, you can withdraw it at any time. Because most of your information stays on your phone, you can also delete it yourself: reset your progress in My progress, or delete the app.

To use any of these rights, email sportlawapps@gmail.com. We will reply within one month. We may ask for details that help us find what you sent (for example, the date of a message), because we do not hold names or accounts.

If you are unhappy with how we have handled your information, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office, the United Kingdom's data protection regulator. We would appreciate the chance to put things right first.

If you live outside the United Kingdom, including in a US state with its own privacy law, you may have similar rights, and we will honour them in the same way. We do not sell personal information. Personalised ads can count as "sharing" under some US state laws; you can opt out by refusing personalised ads in Settings, under Privacy options.

Changes to this policy

If we change how the app uses information, we will update this policy, change the date at the top, and show the new version in the app and on our website. If a change needs your consent, the app will ask for it.
